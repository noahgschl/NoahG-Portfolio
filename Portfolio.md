#  My Portal Into The Technology Universe
I have always been fascinated by computers, since I was a kid. I was always interested in learning how things work and what makes them do what they do. 
As I grew up, I was exposed to computers more, and the satisfaction, the excitement I got from learning more about them was intoxicating. It was like I had something that no one else had, a drive to know the complete ins and outs of everything on the screen and behind it too.

#  Since the beginning of my tech journey, I have hit a few different milestones.
•	While in high school, at the age of 15, I built my first computer. I have modified it since, upgraded it, and I am now writing this portfolio on it.

•	I have worked in IT for the past 2+ years. While in the field, I have learned so much about computers, the way they operate, different operating systems, and they way they all interact. 

•	I have been exposed to many networks, servers, and unique and intricate computer systems and had to work in commercial settings where there is little to no room for error. 
Linux has become a large part of my job, and I work with it regularly. I have grown accustomed to using several of the more popular distros, such as Debian and Ubuntu.

#  What was my biggest challenge?
The biggest challenge that I have faced in and still face is trying to understand the system I am working on or the mechanism I am using. Understanding is the first step to doing something, and when I struggle to understand what I am trying to do, it obviously makes everything much harder. 
#  What moment am I most proud of so far?
My proudest moment that I still think of often is when I built my first computer, this computer, back in high school. I did it using YouTube, old computer parts given to me that would’ve ended up being recycled, and much guess work. But the moment when I plugged the power cable in, hit the on button, and was met with a blue power light and the sound of fans whirring to life was the most revolutionary moment for me. 
That event taught me that I could do things I didn’t think were possible, and that with enough determination and time, I could accomplish great things. 
# What is one thing I will do to improve for the second half of class?
I want to get better at managing my time. It is something most people struggle with, and I especially have. I am not a stranger to procrastination; it’s the reason for many late assignment submissions. Even so, I recognize that with the right habits and systems for myself, I can do a lot more with my time if I really try, and when I am able to stay on track and work hard toward something, that feeling of accomplishment is reward enough. 
I want to have a plan I will carry out for anything I am trying to achieve, whether a large coding project or just a small assignment. If I can game-ify it or turn it into a system, then it is much more likely to be done and done well.
# What is one thing I have learned so far?
One thing I have learned so far is that I need to learn to adapt my thinking when I am finding myself running into a wall. The biggest obstacle I have had with working on and with computers is that nothing is assumed. A computer cannot and will not fill in the gaps. And because humans think differently than computers, we have to bridge the gap in our mind, become more systematic, think more like a computer, to really do well with what we’re trying to accomplish. 
And so you have to adapt, you have to remember the things a computer won’t remind you about, you have to be logical and straightforward. Which is incredibly hard when you are used to talking to other humans who think similarly to you.
